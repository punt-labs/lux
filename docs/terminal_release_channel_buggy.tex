\section{Introduction}

The Display holds one socket to the terminal daemon and many replicas on it.
Releasing a replica sends \texttt{CLOSE} for its channel and forgets the
channel locally, but the daemon may already have \texttt{DATA}, a
\texttt{CLOSE}, or an attach reply in flight for it.  Any frame the Display
cannot place raises an error and the Display drops the whole connection, which
disconnects every other replica.  The property to establish is that releasing
one replica never disconnects another.

This variant omits the released-channel memory: releasing a live replica forgets its channel.

\section{Basic Types}

\begin{zed}
  [CH] \\
  CONN ::= linked | severed \\
  VIEW ::= asked | live | cancelled | released \\
  FK ::= reply | data | close \\
  HEALTH ::= calm | failed
\end{zed}

\texttt{CH} is a channel number, also the identity of the attach request that
will be answered on it; numbering restarts with each connection.  The
Display's view of a channel is \texttt{asked} (attach sent, reply awaited),
\texttt{live} (in \texttt{\_channels}), \texttt{cancelled} (replica released
while \texttt{asked}) or \texttt{released} (live replica released).
\texttt{FK} is the kind of a frame the daemon sends: the attach reply on the
control channel, stream data, or the stream close.  Snapshots are data.

\section{State}

\begin{schema}{Sys}
  conn : CONN \\
  dview : CH \pfun VIEW \\
  reqs, dbound, dfin, toDaemon, usedCh, collateral : \power CH \\
  inflight : \seq (CH \cross FK) \\
  health : HEALTH
\where
  \#~inflight \leq 3 \\
  dfin \subseteq dbound
\end{schema}

\texttt{dview} is the Display's bookkeeping.  \texttt{reqs} are attach
requests the daemon has not yet answered.  \texttt{dbound} are the daemon's
viewer bindings and \texttt{dfin} those it has already sent a
\texttt{CLOSE} for.  \texttt{toDaemon} are \texttt{CLOSE} frames the Display
has sent that the daemon has not yet read.  \texttt{inflight} is the single
ordered byte stream from the daemon: control replies and channel frames share
it, so a reply always precedes the frames of its channel.
\texttt{usedCh} are channels numbered on this connection.
\texttt{collateral} is a history variable: the unreleased replicas
(\texttt{asked} or \texttt{live}) that were dropped by a connection error
triggered by some other channel.  \texttt{health} becomes \texttt{failed}
when any such error occurs at all.

\begin{schema}{Init}
  Sys'
\where
  conn' = linked \\
  dview' = \emptyset \\
  reqs' = \emptyset \\
  dbound' = \emptyset \\
  dfin' = \emptyset \\
  toDaemon' = \emptyset \\
  usedCh' = \emptyset \\
  collateral' = \emptyset \\
  inflight' = \langle \rangle \\
  health' = calm
\end{schema}

\section{Receiving a Frame}

The Display reads the head of the stream.

\begin{schema}{ReceiveReply}
  \Delta Sys \\
  c? : CH
\where
  inflight \neq \langle \rangle \\
  head~inflight = (c? , reply) \\
  c? \in \dom dview \\
  dview~c? = asked \\
  dview' = dview \oplus \{ c? \mapsto live \} \\
  inflight' = tail~inflight \\
  conn' = conn \\
  reqs' = reqs \\
  dbound' = dbound \\
  dfin' = dfin \\
  toDaemon' = toDaemon \\
  usedCh' = usedCh \\
  collateral' = collateral \\
  health' = health
\end{schema}

A live replica that reads the daemon's close answers with its own close (\texttt{RemoteSession.\_notice}), which frees the daemon's binding.

\begin{schema}{ReceiveLateReply}
  \Delta Sys \\
  c? : CH
\where
  inflight \neq \langle \rangle \\
  head~inflight = (c? , reply) \\
  c? \in \dom dview \\
  dview~c? = cancelled \\
  dview' = dview \oplus \{ c? \mapsto released \} \\
  toDaemon' = toDaemon \cup \{ c? \} \\
  inflight' = tail~inflight \\
  conn' = conn \\
  reqs' = reqs \\
  dbound' = dbound \\
  dfin' = dfin \\
  usedCh' = usedCh \\
  collateral' = collateral \\
  health' = health
\end{schema}

The reply to a cancelled attach is consumed.  The daemon opened the channel
and queued its snapshot behind the reply, so the Display closes the channel
\\emph{and remembers it as released}: frames already in flight must be
ignored.

  \Delta Sys \\
  c? : CH
\where
  inflight \neq \langle \rangle \\
  head~inflight = (c? , close) \\
  c? \in \dom dview \\
  dview~c? = live \\
  dview' = \{ c? \} \ndres dview \\
  inflight' = tail~inflight \\
  conn' = conn \\
  reqs' = reqs \\
  dbound' = dbound \\
  dfin' = dfin \\
  toDaemon' = toDaemon \cup \{ c? \} \\
  usedCh' = usedCh \\
  collateral' = collateral \\
  health' = health
\end{schema}



\begin{schema}{ReceiveUnplaceable}
  \Delta Sys \\
  c? : CH \\
  k? : FK
\where
  inflight \neq \langle \rangle \\
  head~inflight = (c? , k?) \\
  \lnot ((k? = reply \land c? \in \dom dview \land
      (dview~c? = asked \lor dview~c? = cancelled)) \lor
    (k? \neq reply \land c? \in \dom dview \land
      (dview~c? = live \lor dview~c? = live))) \\
  conn' = severed \\
  dview' = \emptyset \\
  reqs' = \emptyset \\
  dbound' = \emptyset \\
  dfin' = \emptyset \\
  toDaemon' = \emptyset \\
  usedCh' = \emptyset \\
  inflight' = \langle \rangle \\
  collateral' = collateral \cup
    (\dom (dview \rres \{ live , asked \}) \setminus \{ c? \}) \\
  health' = failed
\end{schema}

The frame names a channel the Display can neither place nor excuse.  The
connection is dropped and every unreleased replica goes with it.

A voluntary disconnect or a daemon restart is not an error: the old
connection's channels are gone and the new one restarts numbering.

\section{Goals}

\paragraph{R1 --- a release never disconnects another replica.}
\[
  collateral = \emptyset
\]
The bad state is \texttt{collateral} $\neq \emptyset$.

\paragraph{R2 --- no spurious protocol error.}
The only frame errors are real ones, and none arises from releases:
\texttt{health} $= $ \texttt{calm}.  In the intact model both R1 and R2
hold in every state; the close sent for a channel the daemon has already
dropped (\texttt{DaemonRejectsClose}) is unreachable too.

\paragraph{R3 --- deadlock freedom.}
\texttt{Disconnect} and \texttt{Reconnect} between them are always enabled, so
the model cannot stop; the check guards against a guard written wrongly.

\section{Fidelity}

R1 is reachable: two replicas are live; the daemon sends data for the first; the first is released; the data arrives, is unplaceable, and the connection (with the second replica) is dropped.
