\section{Introduction}

The recommended design: the idle connection is closed when the last replica is released, and a replica reopened while a cancelled attach for its name is unanswered holds its attach back until that reply is read.

\section{Basic Types}

\begin{zed}
  [CH] \\
  NAME ::= nameA | nameB \\
  CONN ::= linked | severed \\
  VIEW ::= asked | live | ended | cancelled | released \\
  FK ::= reply | fail | data | close \\
  WK ::= areq | aclose \\
  FLAG ::= owing | settled
\end{zed}

\texttt{CH} is a request id, which is also the channel the daemon opens for
it.  \texttt{NAME} is a session name.  A frame the daemon sends is a
success \texttt{reply}, a non-retryable \texttt{fail}, stream \texttt{data}
or the stream \texttt{close}.  A frame the Display writes is an attach
request \texttt{areq} or a channel \texttt{aclose}.  \texttt{owing} is the
Display's flush debt after a release.

\section{State}

\begin{schema}{Sys}
  conn : CONN \\
  dview : CH \pfun VIEW \\
  nameOf : CH \pfun NAME \\
  shadowed, deferred, failedCh, usedCh, collateral, dfin : \power CH \\
  inflight : \seq (CH \cross FK) \\
  wq, sent : \seq (CH \cross WK) \\
  dbound : CH \pfun NAME \\
  owed : FLAG
\where
  \#~inflight \leq 3 \\
  \#~wq \leq 3 \\
  \#~sent \leq 3 \\
  dfin \subseteq \dom dbound
\end{schema}

\texttt{wq} is the Display's write queue, not yet flushed; \texttt{sent} is
on the wire to the daemon; \texttt{inflight} is on the wire to the Display.
\texttt{dbound} is the daemon's viewer bindings, one per channel, each for a
session name; the daemon refuses a second binding for a name on the peer.
\texttt{failedCh} are replicas that received a non-retryable rejection.
\texttt{collateral} is a history variable: unreleased replicas dropped by an
error caused by another channel.  \texttt{shadowed} and \texttt{deferred} are
used only by the variants that need them.

\begin{schema}{Init}

\where

  nameOf' = \emptyset \\

  wq' = \langle \rangle \\

\end{schema}

\section{Operations}

while it owes a flush after a release (the pump's guard).

\section{Goals}

\paragraph{F --- no stuck replica.}  \texttt{failedCh} $\neq \emptyset$ is bad:
every attach in this model could succeed, so a failure is a reopened
terminal stuck for good.

\paragraph{O --- no orphan viewer after quiescence.}  No replica is asked or
live, no flush is owed, yet the daemon still holds a binding for which no
\texttt{aclose} is queued or sent.

\paragraph{C --- no collateral.}  \texttt{collateral} $\neq \emptyset$.

\paragraph{P --- a deferred attach is eventually sent.}  A replica held
behind a cancelled attach must have its attach request actually sent once
nothing blocks its name: leaving the held state is not enough, since the
request could be dropped on the way.  Because \texttt{Disconnect} and
\texttt{Reconnect} keep some transition enabled in every state, deadlock
freedom cannot detect a lost attach, so this is a liveness property checked
in LTL under weak fairness for \texttt{SendDeferred} and \texttt{Flush}:
\[
  \mathbf{WF}(\mathit{SendDeferred}) \land \mathbf{WF}(\mathit{Flush})
  \Rightarrow
  \mathbf{G}\,(c \in deferred \land nameOf~c \notin blocked
    \Rightarrow \mathbf{F}\,(\mathit{areq}_c \in wq \cup sent
      \lor c \in \dom dbound \lor c \notin \dom dview))
\]
where $blocked$ is the names with a cancelled attach outstanding.  The
variant without \texttt{SendDeferred} (\texttt{nosend}) and the variant whose
\texttt{SendDeferred} releases the attach without queuing it
(\texttt{drop}) both violate it.
