luxd-pty: the two designs worth choosing between

Both keep the shells alive without a Display, keep terminal bytes off the Hub-to-Hub links, and let agents read screens headless. They differ in one thing: how the daemon is wired to the Hub.

Display (optional viewer) Hub (luxd) luxd-pty Agent / app Shell process

The shared picture: what both designs agree on

The two options

Option 1: a sidecar service

The Hub owns a private socket to its daemon. herdr's and tmux's shape.

Wires

  • Two protocols. A new PTY protocol on the socket (JSON control + binary attach frames), and a new stream channel on Hub↔Display. The Hub translates between them.
  • The daemon has one client, its Hub. It never sees identity; the Hub attaches the principal to each request.
  • Agent tools are Hub tools that forward over the socket.

Option 2: a Hub client that publishes streams

The daemon connects to the Hub like any applet (DES-063). The stream becomes a general Hub primitive.

Wires

  • One protocol. The Hub's client protocol gains a stream primitive; the daemon and the Display both use it. No PTY-specific protocol.
  • The daemon has an identity and a lease. Its sessions are its scenes; a dropped lease is the ownership rule, as for applets.
  • Keystrokes travel as stream bytes to the owning client, not as interaction events.

Recommendation: Option 2, built in Option 1's order

Option 2 is the better end state. It adds one primitive Lux needs anyway, puts the daemon under the identity and lease rules every other client already follows, and makes the Hub and Display indifferent to where terminals come from. Option 1 is faster to a first demo but leaves two protocols and a split policy to maintain.

The risk in Option 2 is that the Hub-side stream primitive is designed around one consumer. So build the daemon first standing alone, exactly as Option 1's steps 1–3 would: snapshot recipe with tests, a process that owns PTYs and Ghostty, a direct connection for the Display, measured throughput and reconnect. That code is identical in both options. Then, with real numbers, design the stream primitive and connect the daemon as a client. If the primitive turns out to be hard or slow, Option 1 is the fallback and nothing built so far is wasted.

What decides it at the gate

  • Can the Hub forward a terminal stream at several times the spike's 30–45 MB/s without hurting scene replication? If not, the forwarder is the piece to make native, in either option.
  • Does a stream-publishing client fit the Hub's client model without special cases? If it needs more than two, that's a signal for Option 1.
